You're probably wondering

“Why pay for translation? Surely AI does that for free now.”

Machine translation has come a long way, and for getting the gist of something it's genuinely useful. But the gist and a text you can put your name to are two different things.

Fluent isn't the same as right

AI produces smooth, confident English even when it has misread the Dutch. The mistakes don't look like mistakes, so nobody spots them until a client, a reader or a regulator does.

It doesn't know your context

A good translation depends on who will read it, the tone your organisation uses and the terms your sector expects. When the Dutch is ambiguous, I check with you instead of guessing.

Someone has to stand behind it

When a text goes out under your name, someone needs to answer for every word. I take responsibility for my translations, and you can always ask me why I chose a particular word.

Your text stays with one person

Free online tools may store or reuse whatever you paste into them. Your documents stay with me and are handled in confidence.

AI is a useful tool. But the final word should come from someone who understands both languages, and your readers.
